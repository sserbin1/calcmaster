## Educational Content

### How Mortgage Payments Work

Your monthly mortgage payment (often called PITI) consists of four main components:

**Principal:** The portion that pays down your loan balance. Early in your mortgage, this is smaller but grows over time.

**Interest:** The cost of borrowing money. Early payments are mostly interest, gradually shifting toward principal as you pay down the balance.

**Taxes:** Property taxes are typically collected monthly and held in escrow by your lender, then paid annually to your local government.

**Insurance:** Homeowner's insurance protects your property. Like taxes, it's usually escrowed and paid annually by your lender.

### Understanding Your Amortization Schedule

Amortization is the process of paying off your loan through regular payments over time. In a 30-year mortgage at 7% interest, here's roughly how payments break down:

| Year | Principal % | Interest % |
|------|-------------|------------|
| Year 1 | 25% | 75% |
| Year 10 | 40% | 60% |
| Year 20 | 60% | 40% |
| Year 30 | 90% | 10% |

This is why making extra payments early in your mortgage has the biggest impact—you're reducing the principal that accrues interest for decades.

### 15-Year vs. 30-Year Mortgage

**30-Year Mortgage:**
- Lower monthly payments
- More flexibility in budget
- Pay significantly more interest over loan life
- Takes longer to build equity

**15-Year Mortgage:**
- Higher monthly payments
- Lower interest rates (typically 0.5-1% less)
- Build equity faster
- Pay far less interest total

**Example:** On a $400,000 loan at 7% (30-year) vs. 6.5% (15-year):
- 30-year: $2,661/month, $558,036 total interest
- 15-year: $3,484/month, $227,167 total interest
- **Savings with 15-year: $330,869**

### What is PMI?

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is required when your down payment is less than 20% of the home price. It protects the lender if you default on the loan.

**PMI typically costs:** 0.5% to 1% of your loan amount annually, added to your monthly payment.

**How to avoid PMI:**
- Put down 20% or more
- Choose a VA or USDA loan (if eligible)
- Use lender-paid PMI (higher interest rate)
- Piggyback loan (80-10-10 structure)

## FAQ Section

### Q1: How much house can I afford?
A general rule is that your monthly housing costs (including mortgage, taxes, and insurance) shouldn't exceed 28% of your gross monthly income. Your total debt payments shouldn't exceed 36%. Use our affordability calculator for a personalized estimate.

### Q2: What's included in a mortgage payment?
A typical mortgage payment includes Principal (loan repayment), Interest (cost of borrowing), Taxes (property taxes), and Insurance (homeowner's insurance)—often called PITI. If your down payment is under 20%, you may also pay PMI.

### Q3: How do extra payments affect my mortgage?
Extra payments go directly toward your principal, reducing the amount that accrues interest. Even small extra payments can save thousands over the loan's life and shorten your payoff date by years.

### Q4: What credit score do I need for a mortgage?
Conventional loans typically require 620+, FHA loans accept 580+ (3.5% down) or 500+ (10% down), and VA loans have no minimum but lenders usually want 620+. Higher scores get better interest rates.

### Q5: Should I get pre-approved before house hunting?
Yes! Pre-approval shows sellers you're a serious buyer with verified financing. It also helps you understand your budget before falling in love with a home you can't afford.
